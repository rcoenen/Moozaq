# Moozaq website design brief

**Designer handoff · Version 1.0 · October 17, 2026**

**Later decisions override this brief.** The site is live at https://moozaq.com. Build and deploy steps are in `website/README.md`. The owner requires the page to say Moozaq is free, open source, and has no ads, accounts, or subscriptions. That supersedes the pricing note below that forbids "free forever" language. There is no early-access email form. Download links stay placeholders until GitHub release URLs exist.

**Language:** English throughout the website.  
**Scope:** A responsive marketing landing page for the Moozaq app, with Android as the primary platform.  
**Creative direction:** A light, warm interpretation of 1970s design, with modern clarity and generous space.  
**Core idea:** Choose the atmosphere for what you are doing. Let the music fill the room.

This brief defines the product story, audience, page structure, suggested copy, visual direction, interaction behaviour and deliverables. The designer should turn it into a distinctive, coherent website rather than reproduce every suggested sentence or layout literally. The positioning, simplicity, accurate availability and respect for the existing brand are the essential constraints.

## 1. The assignment

Design a website that makes someone want Moozaq in their home.

The visitor should quickly imagine a better version of an ordinary moment: getting into work, making dinner, reading on a rainy afternoon, having friends over, or taking a slow morning at their own pace. The product makes it easy to give that moment a musical atmosphere.

The website should feel like an invitation into those moments. Beautiful mix artwork, a clear product demonstration and short, evocative copy should do most of the work. The app is useful because it asks very little of the listener: find a mood, choose a mix and let it run.

The design must answer four questions without making the visitor work:

1. What is Moozaq? An app for discovering long-form music mixes by mood and playing them on compatible home speakers.
2. Why would I want it? To give whatever I am doing the right atmosphere, without continually choosing another song.
3. What does it feel like to use? Visual, simple, relaxed and deliberate.
4. How do I get it? One clear Android action, matched to the actual release stage.

This is a marketing website, not a redesign of the application or a full music player in the browser.

## 2. Product positioning

### The product in one sentence

**Moozaq helps you choose a mood and fill your room with long-form music mixes.**

### The emotional promise

Your home can feel a little different with the right music. Moozaq makes that change easy.

The listener is usually doing something else. Music supports the activity and the atmosphere rather than requiring constant attention. That does not mean the music is disposable or that people cannot enjoy listening closely. It means the product is designed around the life happening alongside it.

### The practical proposition

Browse distinctive artwork and moods, choose an extended mix, and play it on a compatible Google Cast speaker or speaker group. Android is the primary product story. Long-form mixes give the chosen atmosphere time to develop without the listener having to construct a playlist song by song.

### Internal competitive context

Spotify is often approached through a specific artist, album, song or playlist. YouTube is a rich source of long mixes, but the experience is organised around videos. Moozaq's point of entry is the atmosphere someone wants in their room.

The public website does not need competitor comparisons, feature matrices or negative statements about other services. Show what Moozaq makes possible through its own experience.

### What simplicity means here

Simplicity is a small number of meaningful decisions, clear visual choices and controls that stay out of the way. Dependable playback is a core product requirement, but reliability should be felt through the presentation rather than announced as a headline.

Avoid selling engineering work with phrases such as “rock-solid casting,” “bulletproof connections” or “finally, an app that actually works.” The desired response is: “That looks lovely. I understand it. I would use that.”

## 3. Audience and situations

The primary audience enjoys atmospheric music at home and already has, or is considering, compatible connected speakers. They may already find long mixes on YouTube. They value good taste, visual discovery and an easy way to create a feeling in a room.

Do not frame the audience as audio engineers, power users or people who want to manage a large music database. Nor should the site assume an expensive designer home. The product belongs in ordinary kitchens, bedrooms, workspaces and living rooms.

| Situation | What the person wants | Suitable creative territory |
|---|---|---|
| Working or studying | Settle into something and stay with it | Deep focus, ambient textures, restrained electronic music |
| Making dinner | Give the kitchen energy and personality | Warm grooves, jazz, regional music where the catalog supports it |
| Slow mornings | Ease into the day | Cozy jazz, vintage lounge, coffee and daylight |
| Reading or relaxing | Make a room feel comfortable | Mellow jazz, soft instrumentals, calm soundscapes |
| Friends coming over | Create an atmosphere before people arrive | Lounge, rhythmic grooves, sociable warmth |
| Late evenings | Change the character of the room | Noir jazz, cinematic trip-hop, dim light |
| Exploring a different sound | Follow curiosity without needing specialist knowledge | Psychedelic dub, space-age lounge, Persian jazz and other accurately labelled selections |

The owner's example of cooking Indian food with Indian music expresses the ambition particularly well: music can help create the whole occasion. Indian music is a potential editorial addition, not a confirmed catalog category. Do not invent a mix or use unrelated artwork to imply that it is already available.

## 4. Brand and messaging hierarchy

### Name

Use **Moozaq**, spelled M-O-O-Z-A-Q. Do not use “Muzak,” “Moozag” or a newly typeset approximation of the supplied wordmark.

### Recommended lead message

> **Set the mood. Let it play.**

This is the preferred website hero headline. It states an action and an outcome in familiar language.

### Supporting explanation

> Long-form music mixes for whatever you're getting into. Choose a mood, play it on your home speakers, and make yourself at home.

Keep compatible-device detail close enough to be discoverable; the compatibility section makes the Google Cast focus explicit.

### Existing brand line

> **Find your mood. Let it run.**

Retain this as a secondary brand line, for example near the closing call to action. Avoid placing it next to the hero headline where the two would repeat each other.

The older wordmark handoff includes “Let your mood run.” in some lockups. Use the wordmark without its baked-in tagline and set the chosen website copy separately. Do not mix three competing taglines on one page.

### Message priorities

| Priority | Message | Best way to communicate it |
|---|---|---|
| First | Music for the atmosphere you want | Headline, artwork and everyday situations |
| Second | Extended mixes let you settle into that atmosphere | One clear sentence and real duration labels |
| Third | Play through your home speakers | App demonstration and compatibility copy |
| Fourth | Easy to browse and control | A short, legible interaction sequence |
| Fifth | Android first, other platforms considered separately | Honest availability near the conversion action |

Do not lead with YouTube extraction, network protocols, speaker discovery, source management, technical diagnostics or the development history.

## 5. Voice and copy style

Write in warm, direct English. A little personality is welcome; relentless wordplay is not. Sentences should sound natural when spoken.

Use concrete words such as room, kitchen, morning, work, dinner, music, mood and play. Emphasise what the listener is doing and how the space feels.

Prefer short headlines with enough explanatory copy to make the product clear. Do not rely on poetic slogans alone.

| Use this direction | Avoid this direction |
|---|---|
| “Something's cooking.” | “Elevate your culinary audio journey.” |
| “Music for getting into the zone.” | “Scientifically optimised productivity soundscapes.” |
| “Give the evening a different feel.” | “Transform every moment with revolutionary audio.” |
| “Choose a mix. Let it run.” | “Unlimited seamless entertainment, anywhere.” |
| “Long mixes. Less choosing.” | “The world's most reliable streaming platform.” |

Do not promise better concentration, health benefits, uninterrupted playback, universal compatibility, ad-free listening, unlimited access or free pricing without an approved basis. Do not invent testimonials, ratings, press mentions, download counts or creator partnerships.

## 6. Availability and product truth

This section is for the designer and copywriter. It should govern the design without becoming a large disclaimer on the page.

| Item | Current position | Website treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Android | A standalone development app exists; playback and limited reconnection scenarios have been demonstrated | Primary platform. Public download or early-access CTA depends on release readiness |
| macOS | A working desktop implementation exists | Secondary platform; offer a download only when a distributable release is ready |
| Google Nest and Google Cast | The initial supported playback direction, with hardware testing underway | Main speaker ecosystem in the story; publish a checked compatibility statement |
| Saved speaker groups | Working examples exist, with further reliability validation needed | Show only capabilities available in the featured release; avoid universal whole-home claims |
| iOS | Possible future platform | Optional “iOS — planned” only with owner approval; no App Store badge or promised date |
| Sonos | Possible future integration | Keep out of the supported-device strip; mention as being explored only if useful and approved |
| Apple speaker ecosystem | Possible future integration; precise scope unresolved | Do not imply AirPlay or HomePod support or group them under a vague “Apple supported” badge |
| Pricing | Not decided in this website brief | No invented price, free trial, subscription tier or “free forever” language |
| Catalog | Existing mixes and channel imports, with a one-hour minimum for browsing | Show real titles, creators, artwork and durations; avoid a fixed catalog-size promise |

The phone-independent playback and return-to-session behaviour are intended product qualities with limited successful tests. The website should not turn them into an absolute promise that playback survives every network change, reboot or long session.

The current source route is an experimental implementation. This design assignment does not establish commercial distribution permission or artwork reuse permission. Keep an internal launch checklist for the owner covering the release, content model, featured assets and final claims. Do not add a legal lecture to the landing page.

## 7. Conversion strategy

Create one coherent page with two CTA text variants for different release stages. These are content variants, not two separate visual concepts.

### Design default while public availability is unresolved

**Primary:** Get early access  
**Context:** Android first.  
**Secondary:** Explore the moods

The primary action opens a short early-access form or reaches a dedicated form section. Design email input, submit, pending, success and error states. Collect only what is needed; optional platform interest can be offered after the email step. Do not write “You're on the list” until the submission succeeds.

### Public Android release variant

**Primary:** Get Moozaq for Android  
**Secondary:** Explore the moods

Use an official store badge only when there is a real listing and an approved asset. If distribution is by APK, label the destination accurately rather than presenting it as a store install. The release owner must supply the actual destination and any necessary installation guidance.

macOS may have a secondary text link in the platform section once ready. Do not give Android, macOS, iOS and future speaker integrations equal visual weight.

Repeat the primary CTA in the hero and closing section. A compact header CTA is also appropriate. Avoid adding a conversion button beneath every artwork card.

## 8. Recommended page structure and copy

The following is the recommended narrative order. The designer may combine adjacent sections if the story remains clear. In particular, the mood gallery and everyday situations should not become two repetitive, full-length galleries.

### A. Header

**Purpose:** Establish the brand and make the next step easy.

Use the supplied Moozaq wordmark, restrained anchor navigation and the primary CTA. Suggested anchors: “The moods,” “How it works” and “Your speakers.” On smaller screens, reduce navigation before shrinking the wordmark or CTA excessively.

No account menu, search field, genre filter or app-like control toolbar is needed. If the header becomes sticky, keep it shallow and prevent it obscuring section headings.

### B. Hero

**Purpose:** Explain the product and make its atmosphere desirable in the first viewport.

**Headline:**

> Set the mood. Let it play.

**Supporting copy:**

> Long-form music mixes for whatever you're getting into. Choose a mood, play it on your home speakers, and make yourself at home.

**Actions:** Primary conversion CTA and “Explore the moods.”

**Visual:** A confident composition of real mix posters with one clear Android app view. The artwork provides colour and personality; the app view explains that this is a usable product. The entire hero should not resemble a generic phone mockup floating in a gradient.

Use a small number of readable posters rather than a wall of tiny thumbnails. One or two may sit at slight angles, but important copy and controls remain straight and easy to scan. Maintain a clear quiet area for the headline.

The visitor should see the product explanation and primary action without needing to decode the artwork. Do not autoplay music or video.

### C. Mood gallery and everyday moments

**Purpose:** Let visitors recognise themselves in the product.

**Section heading:**

> What does today feel like?

Present roughly six mood-led editorial choices. The labels below describe situations; they are marketing entry points, not a claim that the app has these exact category names.

| Editorial label | Supporting line | Possible catalog direction |
|---|---|---|
| Find your focus | Music for getting into the zone. | Confirmed ambient or focus mixes |
| Ease into the day | Warm sounds. No rush. | Cozy jazz and vintage swing |
| Something's cooking | Give the kitchen its own rhythm. | Suitable lounge, jazz or groove selections |
| Have people over | Set the atmosphere before the doorbell rings. | Lounge or rhythmic mixes appropriate to the occasion |
| Stay up a little longer | Low lights. A little late-night jazz. | Noir jazz and cinematic trip-hop |
| Drift somewhere else | Follow a sound you haven't found before. | Psychedelic dub or space-age lounge |

Choose the imagery after reviewing actual available mixes. Do not label an energetic or ominous mix as relaxing solely because its cover uses warm colours.

Each editorial card or grouping should show real poster art, a concise mood label and at most one short descriptive line. Mix titles and creator credit can appear below or in the expanded view. Do not layer long text over a poster that already contains substantial lettering.

On the website, selecting a mood may reveal a few related examples or update the product preview. It must not start audio, connect a speaker or imply that a track has actually been sent to the visitor's home.

### D. The longer mix benefit

**Purpose:** Explain the product's format in human terms.

**Heading:**

> Long mixes. Less choosing.

**Copy:**

> Settle into a sound for an hour or more. From the first coffee to the last page, there's room to stay with the feeling.

Pair this with two or three real mix durations and artwork, or a close view of the app's current mix. Keep duration metadata accurate. Most promoted examples can be one to four hours long, but the catalog is not capped at four hours.

This section should be brief. Do not imply an infinite radio service, automatic queue or endless station unless it exists in the release being marketed.

### E. How it works

**Purpose:** Make the experience easy to imagine and remove uncertainty about home playback.

**Heading:**

> Pick a feeling. Fill the room.

Use three simple steps with matching UI crops:

1. **Find your mood.** Browse mixes by the atmosphere you're after.
2. **Choose your speakers.** Pick a compatible Google Cast speaker or saved group.
3. **Press play.** Get back to whatever you were getting into.

Illustrate the actual app: mood artwork, speaker choice, then the current mix and controls. Avoid a complex animated journey through settings. If live group editing is not ready for the advertised Android release, the demonstration must not show adding arbitrary rooms during playback.

Use a real Android screen or a faithful reconstruction of the shipped interface. It is acceptable for the website to be light while the app inside the phone remains dark.

### F. Home speaker compatibility and platforms

**Purpose:** Answer “Will this fit my home?” clearly.

**Heading:**

> At home on your home speakers.

**Suggested copy:**

> Moozaq is being built around Google Nest and compatible Google Cast speakers, with Android as your remote.

For a supported public release, replace the development phrasing with an approved statement of actual compatibility. Show simple device illustrations or approved brand assets, followed by concise Android and macOS availability. Keep future platforms visually separate from current support.

Do not display a row of Google, Apple, Sonos and Bluetooth logos as if all are supported. Do not turn a temporary experimental group capability into a broad “any speakers, any combination” promise.

### G. Short FAQ

**Purpose:** Resolve real questions without overloading the main story.

Design four to six accessible accordion rows. The following answers are draft content for release review.

**What kind of music will I find?**

> Long-form mixes across moods and styles, from cozy jazz and vintage lounge to cinematic trip-hop and psychedelic sounds. Browse the artwork and follow whatever fits your day.

**How long are the mixes?**

> Every mix shown in the browser is at least an hour long. Many run for several hours.

**Which speakers can I use?**

> Moozaq's initial focus is Google Nest and compatible Google Cast speakers. Check the release's supported-device information before installing.

**Where do the mixes come from?**

> Moozaq brings together long-form mixes from YouTube creators, with their original titles, artwork and attribution.

This answer describes catalog discovery. Final playback and source wording must match the approved public content model; do not imply that Moozaq owns or exclusively produces the mixes.

**Can I use Moozaq on iPhone or Mac?**

Use release-specific copy. Android is the focus; a Mac version exists in development. iPhone availability is not committed. Avoid “coming soon” as a substitute for a decision.

**What does it cost?**

Omit this row until there is an approved answer. Do not design a pricing table as part of this brief.

If asked about YouTube Premium, the development direction does not require it. Whether and how that is stated publicly is a release decision; it should not become the headline or a promise about every source.

### H. Closing invitation

**Purpose:** Bring the emotional story back to one clear action.

**Heading:**

> Give your room a different feeling.

**Supporting copy:**

> Find your mood. Let it run.

Repeat the primary Android or early-access CTA. This can be a warmer colour block or a dark navy section using the full neon wordmark. Keep the action readable and the surrounding composition calm.

### I. Footer

Include brand, contact/support and the applicable privacy, terms and artwork/source credits destinations. Include a Mac link only if it has a meaningful destination. Reserve space for the final copyright owner rather than inventing a company name.

Do not add fake social accounts, inactive app-store badges or empty navigation destinations merely to fill the footer.

## 9. Visual direction

### Overall character

Warm, light, tactile and quietly playful. Take inspiration from 1970s record sleeves, domestic hi-fi, printed music posters and rounded typographic forms, then use contemporary spacing, hierarchy and interaction design.

The result should feel current and usable, with a recognisable period influence. Avoid a costume-like retro treatment: heavy distressing, constant wavy backgrounds, novelty lettering everywhere, fake vinyl controls or a brown filter over every image.

### Colour direction

The following are starting points for exploration, not final validated interface tokens:

| Role | Suggested colour | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Main background | Warm ivory `#FFF7E8` | Most of the page |
| Secondary surface | Oat `#F1E5D0` | Cards, forms or quiet section changes |
| Main text | Deep cocoa `#30231D` | High-contrast body copy and headings |
| Accent | Apricot `#FFB568` | Primary actions and selected details, with dark text |
| Secondary accent | Burnt orange `#D2461E` | Small graphic accents, drawn from the brand |
| Supporting tone | Muted olive `#68704B` | Occasional editorial detail |
| Dark anchor | Midnight navy `#0D1322` | App context, final section or neon wordmark placement |

Most of the page should be light. Real poster art brings the richer reds, blues, greens and golds. Avoid giving every section its own competing palette. The designer must check final foreground/background combinations for accessibility; the suggested colours are not a contrast certification.

### Typography

Use one expressive display face with rounded, warm or softly sculpted forms, paired with a clear contemporary text face. Body copy, buttons and captions must remain easy to read. Test real long mix titles, not just short placeholder headlines.

Keep the display face for selected headings. Avoid narrow body fonts, tiny uppercase labels, excessive letterspacing and more than two font families. The existing brand package mentions Jost for live tagline text; this is available as a direction, not a requirement to use its older wording.

The designer should propose a font pairing and confirm licensing and web performance before handoff. Do not redraw the logo using the chosen headline font.

### Layout and shape

Use generous margins, purposeful asymmetry and a clear reading order. Rounded cards and pill-shaped actions can echo the app and the wordmark, without putting every sentence inside a capsule.

Balance a memorable hero with calmer sections below. Prefer a few strong compositions over repeated identical feature-card grids. Use actual poster aspect ratios wherever possible; a wide YouTube mix image should not automatically become a square album cover.

### Texture and photography

A subtle paper texture may work on large backgrounds if it does not interfere with text or add loading cost. Keep app screens crisp. A small amount of domestic photography or illustration may help establish a room, but the product artwork should remain the main visual language.

Avoid generic stock imagery of people wearing headphones. This product's central context is music in a space. Avoid cultural shorthand or stereotyped props when illustrating regional music.

## 10. Brand assets and artwork handling

### Existing brand assets

| Asset | Repository location | Instruction |
|---|---|---|
| Wordmark guidance | `assets/branding/Moozaq-Wordmark/README.md` | Read before applying the logo |
| Flat wordmarks | `assets/branding/Moozaq-Wordmark/svg/` | Navy or black on light backgrounds |
| Neon wordmark | `assets/branding/Moozaq-Wordmark/svg/moozaq-wordmark-neon.svg` | Dark backgrounds, at least 200 px wide |
| Current app icon | `assets/branding/Moozaq-AppIcon-B/` | Use version B; preserve original handoff |
| Current app design | `docs/design-handoff/` | Reference for faithful product screens, not the website's light palette |
| Catalog metadata | `compose-app/androidApp/src/main/assets/catalog.json` | Real titles, creators, categories, durations and thumbnail references |
| Source references | `docs/starter-videos.md` | Editorial leads and source links; older implementation notes are historical |

Preserve wordmark proportions, existing stripe colours and clear space of at least half the height of the “o” on each side. Use a flat version below the neon minimum size; the supplied flat minimum is 80 px. Do not place the neon logo on busy artwork, stretch it or add extra glow and shadow.

### Mix artwork

Use the actual poster art from the selected mixes. Do not generate replacement covers or substitute a brand logo for all examples. Genuine variety is essential: six similar cat covers would incorrectly make Moozaq look like a single cozy-jazz channel.

Create an asset manifest for every featured mix containing its source/video ID, original title, creator, duration, chosen image, editorial mood, crop instructions and publication approval status. Separate visual selection from approval to publish. A cached thumbnail is not an approval record.

Keep creators' lettering and identities intact. Preserve meaningful poster content when cropping, and provide a readable creator credit adjacent to the example or in a clearly accessible credits/detail treatment. Do not relabel a creator's work as an original Moozaq release.

At minimum, the first concept should include coherent examples from cozy jazz, dark jazz/trip-hop, lounge or space-age music, and a contrasting psychedelic or ambient direction. For expanded mood examples, aim for six suitable candidate mixes per mood where the catalog supports that depth; the landing page itself does not need to display them all.

Useful existing references include Sepia Lounge for warm illustrated jazz, Jazz Haze for cinematic nightlife, Noir Tiki Lounge for space-age and spy-lounge imagery, and Futuristic Sound for psychedelic dub. Treat genre and era labels as creator/catalog descriptions until editorially checked, not verified historical provenance.

## 11. Interaction and motion

Motion should make the page feel inviting, not busy. Use subtle image lift, a modest transition between selected moods and short section entrances where useful. Core content must remain readable without animation.

The preferred interactive feature is a silent mood preview: choose an editorial mood and see a few real mix covers and a matching app view. The selected state must be obvious. A labelled next/previous control or direct card choice is preferable to a fast, endlessly moving carousel.

If the design includes a staged app sequence, identify it as a product preview. Its “playing” state is illustrative, not a live speaker connection. Do not simulate successful casting to the visitor's devices.

No music starts on page load, hover, scroll or selecting a mood. Audio preview is outside the initial scope. If added later, it needs an explicit play action, a clear stop control and approved source handling.

Avoid scroll hijacking, automatic snapping, compulsory horizontal scrolling of the whole page, custom cursors and elaborate parallax. Honour reduced-motion settings and keep all essential content available when motion is disabled.

## 12. Responsive design and accessibility

Design the mobile experience with the same care as desktop: the primary audience will install an Android app, and many first visits will be on a phone.

Deliver compositions for approximately 390 px, 768 px and 1440 px widths, plus behaviour notes for narrower 320 px screens and wide desktops. These are review sizes, not rigid implementation breakpoints.

On mobile, put the proposition and CTA before decorative compositions become lengthy. Reflow the hero rather than simply shrinking a desktop collage. Keep artwork recognisable; a horizontal editorial shelf can work when it has a visible continuation cue and usable navigation. Do not make a large sticky download banner obstruct the content.

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the accessibility target. Specify readable contrast, visible keyboard focus, logical tab order, descriptive form labels, error messages associated with their fields, and touch targets around 44 px or larger. Meaning must not depend on colour alone.

Artwork carrying essential meaning needs appropriate text alternatives; decorative duplicate posters should not make a screen reader announce the same titles repeatedly. Mood previews and FAQ accordions must work with keyboard input. Avoid hover-only discovery of essential website actions.

Keep the primary CTA and explanation available at increased text size. Provide reduced-motion variants and clear treatment of form submission failures. Visual QA should include long titles, missing artwork and slower connections.

## 13. Performance and implementation handoff

Design for a fast, usable first visit on a normal phone connection. The main message and CTA should not wait for every image or font to arrive.

Identify the hero's priority images, specify responsive image sizes and lazy-load supporting artwork below the fold. Reserve image dimensions to prevent layout movement. Use outlined SVG brand assets where appropriate, compressed raster artwork and a restrained font payload.

Do not make a high-resolution background video or heavy animation framework essential to the design. Provide a stable fallback for missing artwork: a warm neutral surface with the subdued grayscale app mark where helpful, without presenting it as a real mix cover.

The design handoff should include reusable components, spacing and colour tokens, typography styles, interaction states, image crops and clear responsive rules. The web implementation framework is outside this brief.

## 14. Deliverables

The designer should supply:

1. **A creative direction board** showing palette, typography, composition, representative real artwork and treatment of the existing wordmark.
2. **A complete desktop landing page** with the full narrative and realistic English copy.
3. **A complete mobile landing page**, plus the key tablet transitions and responsive behaviour notes.
4. **A clickable prototype** covering navigation, mood preview, FAQ and the primary conversion flow. It remains silent.
5. **CTA variants** for early access and a public Android release, with secondary macOS availability handled consistently.
6. **An asset and source manifest** for selected artwork, credits, image crops and publication status.
7. **A compact component and token set** for buttons, mood examples, app previews, accordions, forms, navigation and footer.
8. **A developer handoff** covering keyboard behaviour, reduced motion, focus, errors, loading states, responsive rules and exports.
9. **A list of unresolved content decisions**, kept separate from polished page copy.

Keep design files editable and organise them around the final page and reusable elements. Do not substitute a collection of attractive hero concepts for the complete page and mobile experience.

## 15. Review criteria

We will evaluate the design against the following questions:

- Can someone explain what Moozaq is after seeing the hero for a few seconds?
- Does the page evoke everyday atmosphere more strongly than technical music management?
- Are long-form mixes and home-speaker playback understandable?
- Does Android have a clear primary action?
- Does the light 1970s influence feel specific and considered without reducing legibility?
- Is the actual poster art a central part of the identity, with enough variety to convey breadth?
- Does the app demonstration reflect real functionality rather than invented features?
- Are current support, development platforms and future possibilities clearly distinguished?
- Does mobile feel deliberately designed rather than squeezed down?
- Can the page be understood and operated without sound, motion or a mouse?
- Is the overall impression simple, warm and desirable?

A small comprehension check with prospective users should ask what the product does, when they would use it, which devices they think it supports and what they expect the CTA to do. If answers are confused, improve the hierarchy before adding more copy.

## 16. Launch measurement

The main business measure is conversion to the real next step: qualified early-access signup or Android download/store visit, depending on release stage.

Useful supporting measures include hero CTA clicks, closing CTA clicks, mood-preview engagement, visits to compatibility information and form completion. A website store-link click is not evidence of a completed installation; measure actual activation separately where available.

The product's downstream success is a listener finding a mix and starting it on a compatible speaker. Do not optimise the site for scrolling or animation engagement at the expense of that understanding.

## 17. Decisions the owner must settle before publication

These decisions need not delay visual exploration. Use the early-access variant as the design default and keep unresolved claims out of the polished copy.

| Decision | Design default |
|---|---|
| Public Android release, private beta or waitlist? | Early-access CTA until the real destination is supplied |
| Store distribution or direct download? | Neutral branded CTA, no unapproved store badge |
| Is macOS a public offering at launch? | Secondary platform with no active download promise |
| Should iOS be mentioned? | Omit from the main conversion area; no timeline |
| Should Sonos or Apple support be mentioned? | Omit from supported-device presentation |
| Price and commercial model? | No pricing section until approved |
| Which exact devices and group features are supported? | Use a release-checked compatibility statement |
| Which mixes and covers can appear publicly? | Maintain the asset approval manifest |
| Final public source/playback description? | Owner-approved wording consistent with the release model |
| Site domain, contact and legal destinations? | Reserve real content slots, not invented links |

## 18. The creative idea to preserve

Someone opens Moozaq because they want their afternoon, kitchen, workspace or evening to feel a certain way. The website should let them experience that possibility before asking them to learn anything complicated.

The design's job is to make a simple invitation feel compelling:

> **Set the mood. Let it play.**
